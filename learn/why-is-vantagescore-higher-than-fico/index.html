Why Is My VantageScore Higher Than My FICO Score?

Your VantageScore is often higher because it is a different model, kinder to paid collections and thin files. Here is why, and which score lenders actually use.

Alexander Katsman

July 20, 2026

Why Is My VantageScore Higher Than My FICO Score?

Your VantageScore is higher because it is a different model, not because your FICO is broken. VantageScore tends to be easier on paid collections and thin files, so for a lot of people it lands 20 to 60 points above their FICO. The number is real. It just is not usually the one a lender uses.

People see this and panic, assuming one score is a lie. Neither is. FICO and VantageScore are two separate companies scoring the same credit report with different rules, so they land on different numbers, the same way two teachers can grade the same essay differently. The trap is not the gap itself. The trap is walking into a loan application trusting the higher, friendlier number when the lender is about to pull the stricter one.

Here is why the two drift apart, when the gap can flip, and which one to actually plan around.

Why the two numbers drift apart

They read the same report but weigh it differently, and a few specific rules create most of the gap.

VantageScore leans a little more on recent behavior, and it is often more forgiving of paid collections and short credit histories than the FICO version your lender pulls, especially the older mortgage-specific FICO models, which are stricter. There is also a structural reason more people score higher: VantageScore can generate a score from about one month of history, while FICO needs roughly six months of activity first. The chart shows that difference. For thin files, that alone is the difference between a friendly VantageScore and no FICO at all.

When the gap flips the other way

Here is the concession the “VantageScore is inflated” crowd skips: the gap does not always go one direction.

Your VantageScore can also come in lower than your FICO. It reacts faster to some changes, so a recent hard inquiry or a balance spike can ding it more sharply in the short term. So this is not a case of one score being permanently generous and the other permanently harsh. They are just tuned differently, and depending on your file, either one can be on top. That is exactly why chasing whichever number happens to be higher is a mistake.

Which one lenders actually use

This is the part that settles it. About 90 percent of top lenders use a FICO score to make decisions. Your free VantageScore usually is not in that decision at all.

So the higher VantageScore is a fine morale boost and a genuinely useful progress tracker, but it is not the number that approves your mortgage, your car loan, or your card. Plan around your FICO. What to actually do

Stop comparing the two numbers and start improving the report underneath them, because that lifts both, and it lifts the FICO that counts.

Pull all three reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and remove errors first: a paid collection still showing a balance, a wrong late mark, an account that is not yours. Then get your card utilization down before the statements close. Both moves raise your FICO and your VantageScore together.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my VantageScore higher than my FICO score?

Because they are different models built by different companies. VantageScore weights recent behavior a bit more and is often easier on paid collections and thin files, and it can score a one-month-old file that FICO cannot score at all. That tends to push it higher for many people, though the gap can also run the other way.

Which is more accurate, VantageScore or FICO?

Neither is more accurate; they answer the same question differently. What matters is which one your lender uses, and that is almost always a FICO version. So your FICO is the more relevant number for an approval, even if your VantageScore looks nicer.

How big is the gap between VantageScore and FICO?

It varies by person, but a difference of 20 to 60 points is common, and it can be larger on thin or damaged files. The gap is normal and does not mean either score is wrong.

Is the score on Credit Karma my real score?

It is a real VantageScore 3.0, but it is usually not the score a lender pulls to decide your application. Treat it as a good progress tracker, not the number that gets you approved.

Should I worry about the difference?

No, but do not rely on the higher number when you apply for credit. Plan around your FICO, since that is what lenders use. Cleaning up report errors and lowering utilization raises both scores, often by 30 to 60 points, so the fundamentals close the gap in the direction that counts.
