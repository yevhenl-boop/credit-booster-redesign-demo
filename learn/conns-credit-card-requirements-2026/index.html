Conn’s HomePlus Credit Card Requirements in 2026: The Honest Status First

The most important thing to know in 2026 is that Conn’s HomePlus is out of business, and its financing is closed to new customers. Conn’s filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on July 23, 2024, and shuttered all of its stores by late October 2024, ending roughly 134 years of operations. Its in house store credit card and its YES MONEY installment program are gone with it. If you are searching for the conns credit card requirements because you want to finance furniture or electronics, the honest answer is that Conn’s cannot help you today, and this guide points you to lenders that can. It also explains what Conn’s used to require, since Conn’s was one of the better known names for challenged credit financing, and what existing account holders should know.

Before you apply anywhere, it is worth knowing exactly what a lender will see. Our guide to AI credit repair tools covers the apps that pull all three bureau reports and flag the errors dragging a score down.

What Credit Score Was Needed for Conn’s HomePlus Financing?

Conn’s built its business around approving people other lenders turned away. It ran its own in house credit operation, priced for higher risk, and leaned heavily on income and employment rather than a single score. That is why so many shoppers with challenged credit and thin files used Conn’s for furniture, appliances, and electronics.

Below is a realistic view of how Conn’s historically approached approvals by FICO band, when it was still lending. Treat these as informed estimates based on how Conn’s positioned its in house and YES MONEY financing, not a current promise, because the company has wound down.

Who Issued the Conn’s HomePlus Credit Card?

Unlike most retailers, Conn’s did not rely on an outside bank for its store card. It lent in house. Conn’s operated its own lending arm and offered two products: a closed loop store credit card usable only at Conn’s locations, and YES MONEY, an installment financing program marketed to borrowers with limited or damaged credit.

Why this matters:

  • In house lending is why the wind down ended new credit entirely. With no partner bank to carry the program, the bankruptcy closed the lending along with the stores.
  • The accounts were sold, not continued. Conn’s customer accounts and receivables were sold to Jefferson Capital Systems, a debt buyer, in a transaction that closed in December 2024.
  • You still owe the balance if you have one. If you carried a balance, the debt transferred to the new owner, and you are expected to keep paying it. Accounts continue reporting to the credit bureaus.

Which Bureau Did Conn’s Pull, and Hard Versus Soft?

Conn’s ran a credit check on applications, so a full application was a hard inquiry that could cost a few points for a short time. Like most lenders, it did not publicly commit to a single bureau, and the report it pulled could vary.

A few points that still apply if you are shopping for a replacement lender:

  • Soft versus hard inquiry. A prescreened offer can use a soft pull that does not affect your score, while a full application is a hard inquiry. Our guide to the difference between hard and soft credit inquiries explains what each one does.
  • Keep all three reports clean. Since you cannot predict the bureau any lender will pull, challenge and correct errors across Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion before you apply anywhere.
  • Watch your old Conn’s account. If a balance moved to Jefferson Capital, make sure it reports accurately, since an error there can drag your score.

Conn’s HomePlus Requirements: What the Checklist Looked Like

When Conn’s was lending, its requirements leaned on stability more than on a high score. If you are targeting a challenged credit lender now, expect a similar list.

  • Age and residency. At least 18 and a US resident with a valid address.
  • Social Security number or ITIN. Used to pull your credit file and verify identity.
  • Verifiable income and employment. This carried the most weight at Conn’s, which is how it approved lower scores.
  • A down payment on higher risk deals. YES MONEY and similar programs often required money down.
  • Manageable existing debt. Even flexible lenders decline when the payment clearly does not fit the budget. Our credit utilization guide shows how to lighten your file first.

Step by Step: What to Do Instead in 2026

Since Conn’s is closed, the real question is how to finance what you need and rebuild at the same time. Follow this plan.

  1. Pull your credit from all three bureaus. Check Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, confirm your band, and scan for errors, including anything from an old Conn’s account.
  2. Correct any errors first. A wrong late payment or a collection that should have aged off can drop your score a full band and cost you an approval.
  3. Lower your utilization. Pay revolving balances down where you can. For a structured plan, follow our guide on how to raise your credit score by 100 points.
  4. Choose a challenged credit friendly path. A secured card or credit builder account is the strongest way to rebuild. See our roundup of the best secured credit cards for 2026. For the furniture or electronics itself, lease to own retailers and some Synchrony store cards still finance those purchases.
  5. Check for prequalification. A prequalified offer is usually a soft pull and a strong signal, without a wasted hard inquiry.
  6. Apply once and wait. Submit a single application and avoid other credit in the same window.

What to Do If You Are Denied Elsewhere

With Conn’s gone, a denial on a replacement lender is a roadmap, not a dead end. The lender must tell you why in writing.

  • Read the reason codes. They name the specific factors, high utilization, too many recent inquiries, short history, or insufficient income. Fix the named issue first.
  • Wait before reapplying. Give it at least three to six months so a new hard inquiry does not stack on top of the old one.
  • Build a bridge product. A secured card or a credit builder account reported to the bureaus can lift your profile. Our guide on how to get approved for a credit card with bad credit lays out the rebuilding path, and fixing a credit card denial walks through the recovery steps in order.

Tips to Improve Your Approval Odds

Small moves in the weeks before you apply for any card or loan can change the answer.

  • Pay down revolving balances first. Utilization is one of the fastest moving score factors. A realistic 90 day move for most people is 30 to 60 points.
  • Do not open or apply for other credit. Keep your recent inquiry count low for at least six months.
  • Report accurate, higher income. Include all eligible income you can verify, since income carries extra weight with challenged credit lenders.
  • Let young accounts age. A few more months of clean, on time payments helps a thin file. If you are starting from scratch, see how to build credit with no history.
  • Fix errors before, not after. Correcting a wrong item after a denial is too late for that application.

The bottom line on the conns credit card requirements in 2026: Conn’s HomePlus has wound down, its in house and YES MONEY financing are closed, and old balances now sit with Jefferson Capital Systems. Point your effort at a challenged credit friendly lender that is still open, keep utilization low, and fix report errors before you apply.
