Dovly Review 2026: Is Dovly AI Legit and How Does Dovly Credit Repair Compare?

Dovly AI is legit, and Dovly disputes only one of the three credit bureaus. Verified on dovly.com on September 11, 2026, Dovly pulls TransUnion reports and advertises unlimited AI-powered disputes with TransUnion. Equifax and Experian are not covered on any tier.

That scope fact is the whole comparison. The Federal Trade Commission’s guidance on disputing errors on your credit reports states plainly: “You should dispute with each credit bureau that has the mistake.” In the FTC’s Consumer Sentinel Network Data Book 2024, “Credit Bureaus and Information Furnishers” logged 1,353,175 reports, 20.91 percent of all 6,471,708 Sentinel reports and the largest complaint category of the year.

What Is Dovly AI? A Plain-English Breakdown

Dovly AI is an automated credit repair and monitoring app that pulls your TransUnion report, flags items that look inaccurate or unverifiable, files TransUnion disputes on a recurring schedule, and alerts you when the report changes.

Dovly spec block, read from dovly.com and dovly.com/pricing on September 11, 2026:

  • Bureaus disputed: 1 of 3 (TransUnion)
  • Free tier: $0 per month, monthly TransUnion report and score, manual dispute tool
  • Premium price: $8.33 per month billed at $99.99 per year, marked 79 percent off a $39.99 per month list price
  • Premium extras: weekly TransUnion report and score, unlimited AI-powered disputes with TransUnion, $2,000 credit builder tradeline, bill and rent reporting, TransUnion credit lock, $1 million identity theft insurance
  • Advertised score lift: 93 points average for Premium, 38 for free members, both self-reported by Dovly
  • Separate Dovly Money product: free for 7 days, then $19.99 per month

Dovly AI is a hands-off TransUnion dispute service. What Dovly AI is not is a three-bureau repair engine, and that is the gap this comparison turns on.

Is Dovly AI Legit? The Honest Answer

Yes, Dovly AI is legit. Dovly is a real US company, Dovly, Inc., with a team based in Phoenix, paying members, and a working product. Dovly does not promise to erase accurate negative information, and no legal service can, because accurate information stays on your report until it ages off.

Legitimate is not the same as complete. Single-bureau coverage costs points because bureau files diverge. In the CFPB’s December 2025 report to Congress under FCRA Section 611(e)(5), roughly 90 percent of complaining consumers said they had already disputed the item, but Equifax confirmed a prior dispute about 37 percent of the time and TransUnion about 30 percent. That report counted more than 5.6 million complaints from January 2024 to June 2025, almost 4.8 million about credit reporting, and an almost 3,000 percent rise in bureau complaints since January 1, 2020.

Dovly Credit Builder: What It Is and Whether You Need It

The Dovly credit builder is a $2,000 reported tradeline included on Dovly Premium, verified on dovly.com/pricing on September 11, 2026. It adds positive, on-time payment history to a thin file.

The logic is sound. Per myFICO, the official consumer site of FICO, payment history is 35 percent of a FICO Score, the largest of five categories, ahead of amounts owed at 30 percent, length of history at 15 percent, new credit at 10 percent, and credit mix at 10 percent.

Two limits hold the Dovly credit builder back: it reports to a limited set of bureaus, and it sits behind an annual prepay plan. Rent reporting is the alternative, and it is still rare enough to be an edge: FICO reported in March 2026 that about 2.7 million of roughly 77 million US adult renters, or 3.5 percent, have a rental tradeline. Credit Booster AI includes rent reporting to all three bureaus in the $9.99 Plus plan, and a secured credit card works too.

Dovly AI Credit Repair: How the Dispute Engine Works

Dovly AI credit repair runs a monthly cycle against TransUnion: review the report, identify items that look inaccurate, outdated or unverifiable, submit disputes, repeat. Credit Booster AI runs the same cycle against all three bureaus with an approval step before each letter sends.

Neither company sets the deadlines. The Fair Credit Reporting Act does. Under 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A), a credit reporting agency must “conduct a reasonable reinvestigation to determine whether the disputed information is inaccurate” and correct, update or delete it “before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute.” Section 1681i(a)(1)(B) allows no more than 15 additional days if you supply new information, the source of the familiar 45-day figure, and 1681i(a)(6)(A) requires written results within 5 business days of completion.

The CFPB adds that furnishers, the lender or collector that reported the item, “generally must investigate and respond to your dispute within 30 days” too. Escalation is slower: bureaus get 60 days to answer a CFPB complaint and all three recently averaged more than 50 days. See FCRA dispute rights.

Real User Results and Speed

Dovly advertises an average 93-point lift for Premium members and 38 for free members. Credit Booster AI users typically see 30 to 60 points when real errors are removed. No credit repair company can guarantee a specific increase, and guaranteeing one is prohibited under the Credit Repair Organizations Act.

No AI credit repair company publishes independently audited score data, ours included. What is independently measured is what happens once a dispute escalates. In the CFPB’s 2025 Consumer Response Annual Report, companies responded to 99.9 percent of credit or consumer reporting complaints sent to them, and closed 40 percent with non-monetary relief, 51 percent with an explanation, and 0.02 percent with monetary relief. Non-monetary relief is the bucket that includes correcting or deleting a credit report entry.

Where to Dispute If You Use a Single-Bureau Tool

If your tool only reaches TransUnion, you dispute the other two bureaus yourself. The FTC publishes the addresses and states: “You should dispute with each credit bureau that has the mistake.”

  • Equifax Information Services LLC, P.O. Box 740256, Atlanta, GA 30348
  • Experian, P.O. Box 4500, Allen, TX 75013
  • TransUnion LLC Consumer Dispute Center, P.O. Box 2000, Chester, PA 19016

The FTC advises certified mail with return receipt requested. That date matters: the 30-day clock in 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) begins “on the date on which the agency receives the notice of the dispute.” Keep copies, because the CFPB found roughly 90 percent of consumers reported a prior dispute while Equifax confirmed one about 37 percent of the time.
