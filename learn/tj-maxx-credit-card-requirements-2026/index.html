TJ Maxx Credit Card Requirements 2026: The Score You Generally Need

Here is the fast answer on TJ Maxx credit card requirements: there is no officially published minimum, but most approvals for the store TJX Rewards card happen with a FICO score of about 620 or higher, and the card is issued by Synchrony Bank. Fair credit applicants in the low to mid 600s have solid odds, and thinner files sometimes clear in the high 500s with low utilization. The card is issued by Synchrony Bank and is designed for everyday retail shoppers, so it is more forgiving than a premium travel card. Many fair credit applicants in the low to mid 600s get approved, and thinner files sometimes clear in the high 500s when utilization is low and income is steady. The TJX Rewards card credit score you bring matters, but Synchrony also weighs your recent payment history, how much of your available credit you are using, and how many new accounts you have opened lately. This guide breaks down realistic TJ Maxx card approval odds by score band, which bureau Synchrony tends to pull, the inquiry type, and the exact steps to get a yes.

What Credit Score Is Needed for the TJX Rewards Card?

The honest answer is that Synchrony does not advertise a hard cutoff, and approval is a full profile decision rather than a single number. That said, score still drives most of the outcome. The store-only TJX Rewards credit card, which works at TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, Sierra, and HomeSense, sits in the fair credit tier of retail cards. The companion TJX Rewards Platinum Mastercard, which works anywhere Mastercard is accepted, generally asks for stronger credit.

Which Credit Bureau Does Synchrony Pull for the TJ Maxx Card?

This is one of the most common questions, and the accurate answer is that it depends. The TJX Rewards card is issued by Synchrony Bank, the same bank behind many store cards including Amazon, Lowe’s, and others. Synchrony pulls from Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion, and the bureau it uses is not publicly fixed for any single card.

From reported applicant patterns, Synchrony frequently pulls Equifax and TransUnion, with the exact bureau varying by your state of residence and Synchrony’s internal routing at the time you apply. Because you cannot reliably predict which report they will see, the only safe strategy is to make sure all three of your reports are accurate and clean before you apply.

Why this matters for your odds:

  • One error on the report they pull can sink your application. An outdated collection, a late payment that should have aged off, or a duplicate account on the wrong report can cost you the approval even if your other two reports look fine.
  • You cannot choose the bureau. Since Synchrony picks, you have to treat all three as if they will be the one that decides.
  • The score they see may differ from your free app. Lenders often use a FICO model, while many free apps show a VantageScore. Your real number with Synchrony can read a little higher or lower than the one on your phone.

If you spot a mistake on any report, dispute it before applying. Because the rules and timelines differ by agency, our guide on how credit report disputes work by bureau helps you target the right one quickly.

Hard or Soft Inquiry? What to Expect When You Apply

Submitting a real application for the TJX Rewards card creates a hard inquiry on the bureau Synchrony pulls. A hard inquiry typically trims a few points off your score and fades over several months. Synchrony does not publish a dedicated prequalification tool for the TJX Rewards card the way some issuers do for other products, so you should assume any direct application is a hard pull, not a soft check.

The practical takeaway is to apply only when your profile is genuinely ready, because a denial still leaves the hard inquiry on your report. If you want to understand the difference and how each affects your score, read our breakdown of hard versus soft credit inquiries before you fill out the form. Spacing out applications and avoiding a cluster of new inquiries in the weeks before you apply both help your odds.

TJ Maxx Credit Card Requirements: The Full Checklist

Beyond your score, here is what Synchrony looks for when deciding a TJ Maxx card approval.

  • Age and identity. You must be at least 18 and provide a valid Social Security number or accepted taxpayer ID, plus a government issued ID.
  • Steady income. There is no published income floor, but you must report income that supports the requested credit. Consistent income reassures the lender you can repay.
  • A US mailing address. A residential address in good standing helps, since the card is a US retail product.
  • Low recent credit utilization. Carrying high balances on existing cards is one of the most common reasons fair credit applicants get denied. Aim for under 30 percent, and under 10 percent is even better.
  • Clean recent payment history. A missed payment in the last 6 to 12 months hurts more than older blemishes. Recent on-time behavior matters most.
  • Limited recent inquiries and new accounts. Several new applications in a short window signals risk. Slow down before applying.

Synchrony complies with the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and the Fair Credit Reporting Act, so if you are denied, they must send an adverse action notice that states the exact reasons, plus the bureau and score they used. Keep that letter. It tells you precisely what to fix.

Step-by-Step: How to Get Approved for the TJ Maxx Card

Do not guess. Follow this plan to give yourself the best shot at a TJ Maxx card approval.

  1. Pull all three of your credit reports. Use a free service and review Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, since you do not know which one Synchrony will use. Confirm your score band and flag any errors.

  2. Fix errors and lower your utilization first. Pay revolving balances down to under 30 percent of each card’s limit, ideally under 10 percent, in the statement cycle before you apply. This single move can lift your score quickly. Our credit utilization guide shows exactly how to time payments so the lower balance reports.

  3. Make sure your recent history is clean. If you have a late payment in the last several months, wait until you have a longer run of on-time payments before applying.

  4. Decide which card to request. If your score is in the fair range, apply for the store-only TJX Rewards card, which is easier to get. If you are 670 or higher with a healthy profile, you can target the TJX Rewards Platinum Mastercard for broader use.

  5. Apply in store or online. You can apply at the register during checkout at TJ Maxx, Marshalls, or HomeGoods, or online. Have your income, address, and ID details ready. Expect the hard inquiry.

  6. Use it lightly and pay in full. After approval, keep your reported balance low and pay on time every month. Synchrony reviews accounts and often raises limits for responsible users, which improves your utilization and your score over time.

What to Do If Your TJ Maxx Card Application Is Denied

A denial is not the end. It is a roadmap. Here is how to recover.

  • Read the adverse action notice. Synchrony mails it within a few days and lists the specific reasons, plus the bureau and score they used. This is the single most useful document you will get.
  • Attack the named reasons. If the notice cites high utilization, pay balances down. If it cites recent missed payments, build a clean streak. If it cites too many recent inquiries, pause new applications.
  • Dispute any errors on the report they pulled. Fixing a wrongly reported late payment or a collection that should have aged off can move you a full tier.
  • Consider a secured card while you rebuild. A secured card reports to the bureaus and rebuilds positive history with little risk. See our roundup of the best secured credit cards to choose one that reports to all three bureaus.
  • Wait before reapplying. Give yourself several months of improvement before another hard pull. Reapplying immediately after a denial rarely changes the outcome and stacks inquiries.

If your denial came from thin credit rather than damaged credit, the fix is to add positive history first. Our guide on how to get approved for a credit card with bad credit walks through the exact steps, and the broader how to fix a credit card denial guide covers what to do after a no.

Tips to Improve Your TJ Maxx Card Approval Odds

Small moves in the weeks before you apply can flip a borderline file into an approval.

  • Lower utilization before the statement closes. Card balances usually report on the statement date, not the due date. Pay down balances a few days before the statement cuts so the lower number reports to the bureaus.
  • Do not open other accounts right before applying. Each new account and inquiry is a small ding and a risk signal. Keep your file quiet for a month or two.
  • Keep old accounts open. Length of credit history helps. Closing your oldest card can shorten your average age and raise utilization at the same time.
  • Report all your income accurately. Include eligible household income you have a reasonable expectation of access to, which can support a higher limit.
  • Apply for the store card first if you are unsure. The store-only TJX Rewards card is the easier approval. Once you have a strong on-time record, Synchrony may upgrade you to the Mastercard.

The TJX Rewards Card vs the Platinum Mastercard

It helps to know which product you are actually applying for, because the requirements differ.

The store TJX Rewards credit card works only at TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, Sierra, and HomeSense. It is the easier card to get and the right target for fair credit applicants. The TJX Rewards Platinum Mastercard works anywhere Mastercard is accepted and generally needs a stronger profile, often a score of 670 or higher with low utilization and clean recent history. Both earn rewards in the TJX family of stores, and both are issued by Synchrony. A useful fact: Synchrony can approve you for the store card even when you do not qualify for the Mastercard, so a Mastercard application that comes back as a store-card offer is common.

Both versions report to the bureaus, so either one can help you build credit when used responsibly. If your goal is a card you can use everywhere, build toward the Mastercard. If your goal is to shop the TJX brands and rebuild, the store card gets you there faster.
