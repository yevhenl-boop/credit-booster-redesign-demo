Quick Steps to Remove Hard Inquiries From Your Credit Report

To remove a hard inquiry from your credit report, dispute the pulls you never authorized with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Under 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) each credit bureau must conduct a reasonable reinvestigation free of charge within 30 days of your notice, and under 1681i(a)(5)(A) must promptly delete any item the reinvestigation does not support.

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York recorded 83.021 million hard inquiries in the six months ending in 2026 Q2, up from 79.815 million in 2026 Q1, in its Household Debt and Credit Report data file, Page 5 Data.

TL;DR: Pull all three reports from AnnualCreditReport.com, flag every hard inquiry you never consented to in writing, dispute those online and by certified mail, and diary day 30. FICO states that authorized inquiries stop affecting FICO Scores after one year anyway.

How Do You Get Hard Inquiries Off Your Credit Report?

You get hard inquiries off your credit report by disputing pulls made without a permissible purpose. The Fair Credit Reporting Act, at 15 U.S.C. 1681b(a)(2), names one permissible purpose as acting “in accordance with the written instructions of the consumer to whom it relates,” and 15 U.S.C. 1681b(f) bars a person from using or obtaining a consumer report unless “the consumer report is obtained for a purpose for which the consumer report is authorized to be furnished under this section” and that purpose is certified under section 1681e.

The procedure is identical at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion:

  1. Find the hard inquiry on your report and confirm you never applied and never gave written instructions.
  2. File with the credit bureau online, by phone, or by certified mail.
  3. State that the pull lacked a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. 1681b(f).
  4. The bureau notifies the furnisher within 5 business days under 1681i(a)(2)(A), then reinvestigates within 30 days under 1681i(a)(1)(A).
  5. If the reinvestigation does not support the hard inquiry, 1681i(a)(5)(A) requires prompt deletion.

Send everything at once, because 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(B) lets the bureau extend the 30-day period by “not more than 15 additional days” if you supply information mid-investigation.

What Are Hard Inquiries and Why Remove Them?

A hard inquiry is a record showing a lender requested your credit file. The Federal Reserve Bank of New York defines the category as follows: “Only ‘hard pulls’ are included, which are voluntary inquiries generated when a consumer authorizes lenders to request a copy of their credit report.” That definition excludes creditor reviews of existing accounts and your own checks.

FICO publishes the scoring impact directly. FICO states that hard inquiries stay on the report for up to two years, that “they only affect the FICO® Scores for a year,” and that “for most people, one additional credit inquiry will take less than five points off their FICO Scores.” FICO also states that inquiries “play a minor part in only 10% of what makes up a FICO Score.”

An unauthorized hard inquiry is a different problem. A pull without a permissible purpose violates 15 U.S.C. 1681b(f), and is often the first visible trace of identity theft. The Federal Trade Commission logged 1,135,291 identity theft reports in 2024, 17.5 percent of all 6,471,708 reports filed with the Consumer Sentinel Network. Of those, 406,110 involved a new credit card account.

Soft inquiries sit outside the New York Fed hard pull definition and are not worth disputing. See the difference between hard and soft inquiries.

Step-by-Step Guide: How to Dispute Hard Inquiries

Disputing a hard inquiry costs nothing, because 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) requires the reinvestigation to be conducted “free of charge.” Run the sequence at every bureau showing the pull, because each credit bureau holds a separate file and deletes only from its own.

1. Pull Your Free Credit Reports

Request reports from Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion at AnnualCreditReport.com. Open the section labelled “hard inquiries” or “requests viewed by others” and record the company name and exact pull date, because those two fields are what your letter must name. Our guide on getting your free credit report covers the access routes.

2. Spot the Unauthorized Ones

Test each hard inquiry against the statute, not against memory. The question is whether you gave written instructions under 15 U.S.C. 1681b(a)(2), or the puller had another permissible purpose under 1681b(a)(3). If neither holds, the pull is disputable.

3. Gather Your Evidence

Attach documentation, because a bare assertion travels poorly. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau reported in its December 2025 FCRA Section 611(e)(5) report that around 90 percent of complaining consumers say they already disputed, yet Equifax confirmed a prior dispute about 37 percent of the time and TransUnion about 30 percent. Attach the report page with the hard inquiry circled, an IdentityTheft.gov report if fraud is involved, and any certified mail receipt. If the pulls trace to stolen identity, use our identity theft credit repair process.

5. Craft and Send Your Hard Inquiry Removal Letter

Mail a hard inquiry removal letter alongside the online filing. The Federal Trade Commission advises consumers to “send your letter by certified mail and pay for a ‘return receipt’ so you have a record the credit bureau got it.” That receipt is your dated proof of a prior dispute.

[Your Name] / [Address] / [Last 4 of SSN] / [Date]
[Equifax / Experian / TransUnion] / [Bureau Mailing Address]

Re: Unauthorized Hard Inquiry, Report #[Your Report ID]

I dispute the hard inquiry by [Company Name] dated [MM/DD/YYYY].
I did not apply for credit with this company and gave no written
instructions under 15 U.S.C. 1681b(a)(2). No permissible purpose
existed, and obtaining a report without one is prohibited by
15 U.S.C. 1681b(f).

Please reinvestigate within 30 days under 1681i(a)(1)(A), delete
the item under 1681i(a)(5)(A) if unsupported, and send written
results under 1681i(a)(6).

Enclosed: report page with the inquiry circled.

[Signature] / [Printed Name]

Send to every credit bureau showing the pull, and copy the company that pulled your file. Our credit dispute letter templates cover other items.

6. Track and Follow Up

Diary day 30 from receipt, and day 45 as the outer limit under 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(B). Under 1681i(a)(6) the credit bureau must send written results “not later than 5 business days after the completion of the reinvestigation.” If the hard inquiry returns verified, refile with documents and escalate to the CFPB, which reported in December 2025 that the bureaus have 60 days to answer a complaint and all three averaged more than 50 days.

7. Lock It Down and Prevent More

A credit freeze is free at each credit bureau and blocks new hard pulls until you lift it, and our guide on how to freeze your credit covers all three. Use pre-qualification offers, which fall outside the New York Fed hard pull definition.

Real-Life Examples: Hard Inquiries Removed

Hard inquiry disputes fall into three documented patterns. The scenarios are illustrative; the counts come from the FTC Consumer Sentinel Network Data Book 2024.

Pattern 1: New-account fraud. A hard inquiry appears from a company the consumer never contacted, followed by a new account. The FTC recorded 406,110 new-account credit card identity theft reports in 2024, up 7 percent year over year.

Pattern 2: Loan or lease fraud. A hard inquiry appears tied to a loan never applied for. The FTC logged 176,400 loan or lease identity theft reports in 2024, including 95,689 business or personal loan and 60,188 auto loan or lease reports.

Pattern 3: The pull with no permissible purpose. No account follows, only the inquiry. Under 15 U.S.C. 1681n(a)(1)(B), a natural person who obtains a consumer report under false pretenses or knowingly without a permissible purpose is liable for “actual damages sustained by the consumer as a result of the failure or $1,000, whichever is greater.”

Your Legal Muscle: FCRA Rights

The Fair Credit Reporting Act gives you four enforceable rights on a disputed hard inquiry, each with a named section and deadline.

Your rightStatuteThe obligation
Free reinvestigation in 30 days1681i(a)(1)(A)Reasonable reinvestigation, free of charge, before the 30-day period from receipt ends
Furnisher must be told1681i(a)(2)(A)Notify the furnisher before the 5-business-day period expires
Deletion if unsupported1681i(a)(5)(A)Promptly delete or modify the item based on the results
Written results1681i(a)(6)Written notice not later than 5 business days after completion

On money, 15 U.S.C. 1681n(a)(1)(A) provides for “any actual damages sustained by the consumer as a result of the failure or damages of not less than $100 and not more than $1,000” for willful noncompliance, and 1681n(a)(3) adds “the costs of the action together with reasonable attorney’s fees.” Our explainer on your FCRA rights and how to use them covers the wider picture.

The CFPB reported that in 2025, credit or consumer reporting complaints closed 40 percent with non-monetary relief, 51 percent with an explanation, 10 percent administratively, and 0.02 percent with monetary relief. Deletion is the non-monetary relief you are aiming at.

Long-Term Credit Health Beyond Inquiries

Hard inquiries are the smallest lever on a credit score. FICO publishes the five categories as payment history 35 percent, amounts owed 30 percent, length of credit history 15 percent, new credit 10 percent, and credit mix 10 percent. Hard inquiries sit inside the 10 percent new credit slice, so nine tenths of your score is untouched by them.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get hard inquiries off your credit report?

Dispute the pulls you never authorized with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Under 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) the credit bureau must reinvestigate free of charge within 30 days, and under 1681i(a)(5)(A) must promptly delete an unsupported item.

How long do hard inquiries stay on my credit report?

FICO states that hard inquiries stay on the credit report for up to two years, but that “they only affect the FICO® Scores for a year.”

Can I remove legitimate hard inquiries early?

No. A hard inquiry you authorized in writing is accurate information, and accurate information cannot be removed on request. FICO counts inquiries for one year, though they stay visible for up to two.

What is the fastest way to remove hard inquiries from a credit report?

Filing online is fastest, because the 30-day clock in 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) begins the day the agency receives notice. The FTC publishes the bureau lines as Equifax (866) 349-5191, Experian (888) 397-3742, TransUnion (800) 916-8800.

Do multiple hard inquiries in a short time hurt my score more?

Not necessarily. FICO states that “FICO® Scores group multiple hard inquiries that are made within a short time frame (14 to 45 days) into one inquiry.”

Do I need a credit repair service to remove hard inquiries?

No. 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A) requires the reinvestigation to be free of charge, so a self-filed hard inquiry dispute costs nothing beyond certified postage.

What if the bureau says the inquiry is verified but I know it is wrong?

Refile with documentation, then escalate to the CFPB, which reported in December 2025 that Equifax confirmed a prior dispute about 37 percent of the time and TransUnion about 30 percent.

Does removing a hard inquiry raise my credit score?

Only slightly. FICO states that “for most people, one additional credit inquiry will take less than five points off their FICO Scores,” and no source publishes a guaranteed gain. A pull without a permissible purpose violates 15 U.S.C. 1681b(f).
