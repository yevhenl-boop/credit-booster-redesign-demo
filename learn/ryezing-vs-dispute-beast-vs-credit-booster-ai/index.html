Ryezing vs Dispute Beast vs Credit Booster

Ryezing is $39.99 a month after a $1 trial. Dispute Beast software is free but needs monitoring from $49.99 a month. Verified spec table plus CROA facts.

Alexander Katsman

September 12, 2026•10 min read

Ryezing costs $1 for a seven day trial, then $39.99 a month, and generates dispute letters you print and mail yourself. Dispute Beast charges $0 for its software but generates nothing without an active third party credit monitoring subscription starting at $49.99 a month, plus a separate Sprint Mail charge if you want letters mailed for you.

That is the whole difference. Below is every field we could verify from each vendor’s live pages on 11 September 2026, plus the axis nobody compares these tools on: what the Credit Repair Organizations Act requires of anyone selling a paid credit product.

How each one files disputes

None of these three files a dispute with a credit bureau for you. All three generate a document you send. Understand that before you pay any of them, because the FCRA dispute process is free and open to you directly.

Ryezing generates letters and hands them to you. Its compare page describes a “Dispute Hub” that “generates custom dispute letters for each credit bureau and tracks your progress,” and its about page says what comes next: “you can print and mark dispute letters as sent.” The tracking is manual, and no mailing service appears anywhere on ryezing.com.

Dispute Beast pre-selects, then mails for a fee. Its pricing page says that “after our smart engine pre-selects the accounts on your credit, our AI curates precise verbiage to navigate credit disputes effectively with the bureaus and creditors.” Delivery forks: Sprint Mail sends by USPS First-Class Mail with status written back to your portal, or you download the letters free and mail them yourself. Dispute Beast also claims letters to original furnishers and secondary agencies, not just the three bureaus.

Credit Booster AI runs AI report review and supplies letter templates in the app. Our service agreement states it plainly: draft letters “are produced on your direction, reviewed and authorized by you, and sent in your name.”

The clock is the same for all three. FCRA Section 611, at 15 U.S.C. 1681i(a)(1)(A), requires a consumer reporting agency to reinvestigate free of charge “before the end of the 30-day period beginning on the date on which the agency receives the notice,” extendable by 15 days if you send more information. Section 1681i(a)(6)(A) then requires written notice of the results within five business days of completion. No software shortens that.

Cancellation terms compared

Ryezing’s FAQ states: “You may cancel your membership at any time, through the website, mobile app or by calling Ryezing Customer Support.” Reactivation is phone only.

Dispute Beast’s pricing page states: “Enjoy the freedom to better your credit with no strings attached, cancel our services at any time without penalty.” Note what you cancel. The software is free, so the recurring charge is the third party monitoring Dispute Beast says it does not own or operate, and cancelling it stops the AI from working too.

Credit Booster AI subscriptions run through the App Store and Google Play and cancel in account settings.

What CROA actually requires of a paid credit tool

This axis separates these products more than price does, and it is citable to statute.

A “credit repair organization” is defined at 15 U.S.C. 1679a(3)(A) as any person who uses interstate commerce or the mails to sell a service, for money, “for the express or implied purpose of … improving any consumer’s credit record, credit history, or credit rating” or advising on it. The exclusions in subparagraph (B) are narrow: 501(c)(3) nonprofits, creditors restructuring their own debt, and depository institutions and credit unions. Software is not carved out by name, so whether a DIY tool falls inside is decided by that text, not by a marketing page.

If a tool is inside the definition, four things follow.

A written contract is mandatory. 15 U.S.C. 1679d(a) bars providing services “unless a written and dated contract … has been signed by the consumer.” Subsection (b) requires the total of all payments, a detailed description of the services including all guarantees of performance, an estimated completion date, and the organization’s name and address.

You get three business days to walk away. 15 U.S.C. 1679e gives the consumer the right to cancel “before midnight of the 3rd business day which begins after the date on which the contract … is executed,” and that notice must appear conspicuously in the contract.

Advance fees are banned. 15 U.S.C. 1679b(b) is one sentence: “No credit repair organization may charge or receive any money or other valuable consideration for the performance of any service … before such service is fully performed.”

Results claims are policed. 15 U.S.C. 1679b(a)(3) makes it unlawful to “make or use any untrue or misleading representation of the services of the credit repair organization.”

That last pair has teeth. In CFPB v. Progrexion Marketing and PGX Holdings, the District of Utah ruled on 10 March 2023 that the defendants violated the Telemarketing Sales Rule ban on upfront fees. The court entered a stipulated judgment on 30 August 2023 imposing $2,660,926,481 in consumer redress, civil penalties of $45,817,452 against Progrexion and $18,408,726 against Lexington Law, and a ten year telemarketing ban.

Now apply it. Ryezing bills $39.99 monthly in advance and, as of 11 September 2026, we could not find published terms at all. Dispute Beast publishes full terms opening with the consumer rights disclosure required by 15 U.S.C. 1679c, and its recurring charge sits with a third party monitor. Credit Booster AI’s terms quote the 1679a(3) definition, state that CreditBooster.ai is not a credit repair organization because the consumer performs every action, and state that it “does not guarantee any specific credit score outcome, the removal of any item from a credit report, or any other result.”

The 110% guarantee, read line by line

Dispute Beast promises a 110% money back guarantee if your credit does not improve after a year. The published terms list eight criteria and say you “MUST meet ALL” of them.

  1. Send nine attacks, each within a 40 to 50 day window.
  2. Keep Beast Credit Monitoring or ProCreditWatch active all 12 months, no chargeback.
  3. Use SprintMail for every attack, no chargeback on any SprintMail payment.
  4. Take on no new late payments, negative accounts or installment loans.
  5. Reach total utilization at or below 6% by the eighth attack.
  6. Hold utilization at or below 6% when you file the claim.
  7. Wait 60 days after the ninth attack before submitting.
  8. Prove it with Beast or ProCreditWatch reports only.

Do the calendar arithmetic. Nine attacks means eight intervals: 320 days at the fastest permitted 40 day cadence, 400 at the slowest 50 day cadence. Add the mandatory 60 day wait and the earliest possible claim date is day 380, with day 460 at the other end, and the claim must be filed within 30 days of that wait closing.

Note requirement 3. The guarantee only exists if you pay for SprintMail on all nine rounds, at a price Dispute Beast does not publish. The free mail-it-yourself route disqualifies you.

None of this is hidden. It is on the /110guarantee page, worth reading before it does any work in your decision. Dispute Beast publishes its own disclaimer alongside its score figures: “Results vary. Individual outcomes depend on credit profile, bureau response, and user participation.”

What none of these three can do

No tool here can remove accurate negative information. The disclosure Congress wrote into 15 U.S.C. 1679c says so directly: “neither you nor any ‘credit repair’ company or credit repair organization has the right to have accurate, current, and verifiable information removed from your credit report.” The same statute adds that a credit bureau “must remove accurate, negative information from your report only if it is over 7 years old.” The FTC’s Fixing Your Credit FAQs put it from the other direction: “Anything a credit repair company can do legally, you’ll be able to do for yourself for little or no cost.”

The question is not which tool is most powerful. It is whether the inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable items on your report are worth $40 to $60 a month to work through faster. If your report is accurate, the answer is no, and the fix is time, payment history and utilization.   • Dispute Beast: software $0, monitoring $49.99 (VantageScore 3.0) or $59.99 (FICO 8) a month, so $599.88 or $719.88 in year one plus unpublished Sprint Mail fees.
  • Credit Booster AI: Plus $9.99, Pro $29.99, Max $99.99 a month. Seven day free trial on Plus only, no free tier, one bureau on Plus and all three from Pro.
  • The mailing question decides it. Dispute Beast alone prints and mails, as a paid add-on priced where you cannot see it before signing up.
  • The guarantee has eight conditions, including nine paid SprintMail rounds and utilization at or below 6%, earliest claim day 380.
  • CROA follows the 15 U.S.C. 1679a(3) definition, not a marketing page. Check for published terms, a stated cancellation right and no results guarantee before entering a card number.
  • Bureaus get 30 days under 15 U.S.C. 1681i, plus 15 if you supply more information. No app changes that clock.

Credit Booster AI is a software subscription that reads your report and drafts letters you send yourself. It cannot remove anything accurate, it does not promise a number, and if your report has no errors, you do not need us.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ryezing or Dispute Beast cheaper?

Ryezing is cheaper on paper. Dispute Beast charges nothing for its software, but it only works with an active third party credit monitoring subscription starting at $49.99 a month, so twelve months costs $599.88. Ryezing charges $1 for a seven day trial and then $39.99 a month, which is $480.88 across the first year. Dispute Beast adds a separate Sprint Mail charge unless you print and mail letters yourself.

How much does Ryezing cost?

Ryezing costs $1 for a seven day trial and then $39.99 a month, billed monthly with no contract, according to the pricing page and FAQ page on ryezing.com pulled on 11 September 2026. The Preferred Plan includes monthly three bureau reports and scores, a dispute hub that generates letters you print yourself, rent reporting, utility reporting and goal tracking. Ryezing states you can cancel through the website, the app, or by phone.

Is Dispute Beast really free?

No, not in practice. The app itself is $0 and letter generation is unlimited, but the AI cannot read your file without an active three bureau monitoring subscription. Dispute Beast's own pages state that Beast Credit Monitoring starts at $49.99 a month for VantageScore 3.0 and ProCreditWatch is $59.99 a month for FICO 8, and that both are independent third party services you subscribe to and pay for separately.

What does the Dispute Beast 110% money back guarantee require?

Dispute Beast's published guarantee terms list eight conditions you must meet in full. You must send nine attacks spaced 40 to 50 days apart, use SprintMail for every one, keep monitoring active with no chargeback, take on no new late payments, negative accounts or installment loans, and hold total credit utilization at or below 6% by the eighth attack and again at claim time. A 60 day wait follows the ninth attack.

Can any of these tools remove accurate negative information?

No. The Credit Repair Organizations Act disclosure at 15 U.S.C. 1679c states that neither you nor any credit repair company has the right to have accurate, current and verifiable information removed from your credit report. The FTC's Fixing Your Credit FAQs say the same. Most accurate negative information stays seven years and bankruptcy stays ten. Disputes only move items that are inaccurate, incomplete or unverifiable.
