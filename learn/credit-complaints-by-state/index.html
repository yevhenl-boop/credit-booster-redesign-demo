Credit Complaints by State: Georgia Files 27x More Than South Dakota

Georgia filed 33.93 credit reporting complaints per 1,000 residents in 2025. South Dakota filed 1.26. We mapped all 51 jurisdictions, and average credit score explains less than half of the gap.

Alexander Katsman

July 16, 2026

Georgia has under a third of California’s people and nearly as many complaints

You’d assume credit trouble is spread fairly evenly across the country. Some states poorer, some richer, but nothing wild.

It isn’t even close to even.

A Georgian is 27 times more likely to file a credit reporting complaint than a South Dakotan. Georgia filed 33.93 complaints per 1,000 residents in 2025. South Dakota filed 1.26. The national rate is 14.05.

The raw counts land the same point. Georgia filed 383,484 credit reporting complaints in 2025. California has 3.5 times Georgia’s people. It filed 440,292. So Georgia produced 87% of California’s complaint volume with 29% of its population. Against New York it’s starker: Georgia filed 1.44 times New York’s total with a little over half the people.

What we measured

Complaints come from the CFPB’s public file, filtered to credit reporting products, Date received in calendar 2025. Population comes from the Census Bureau’s Vintage 2025 estimates, the July 1 2025 figure. We divided one by the other. That’s it.

We checked the population file rather than trusting it: the 50 states plus DC sum to the published US total of 341,784,857 exactly, delta zero.

Now the honest limit on that denominator. It’s total population, not credit-active adults. States differ in age structure and in the share of adults who even have a credit file. A per-credit-file rate would be better. But no authoritative state-level count of credit files exists, so we used the reproducible second-best and labeled it rather than implying precision we don’t have.

Puerto Rico is excluded from the map. It’s in the Census file, it isn’t a state, and it would distort the ramp.

Credit scores explain 46% of it. We can’t explain the rest.

The obvious answer is that people with worse credit complain more. We tested it. It’s half right, and the half that’s wrong is the interesting half.

Correlate each state’s 2025 complaint rate against its average FICO score across all 50 states and you get r = -0.675, so r² = 0.456. Lower average score, more complaints. Real, and in the direction you’d expect. Average credit score explains about 46% of the variance between states.

But it cannot explain the size:

  • Average FICO across states runs 680 to 742. That’s a 1.09x spread.
  • Complaint rates run 1.26 to 33.93. That’s a 27x spread.

A 9% spread in credit quality sits next to a 2,600% spread in complaint rates. Credit quality is clearly involved. It’s just as clearly not doing most of the work.

We don’t know what is. That’s the finding, and we’d rather publish it than invent a story that sounds better. Correlation isn’t causation and this page makes no causal claim at all.

So here’s what we can offer instead, sourced rather than speculated. The CFPB itself attributes complaint growth partly to “third parties using the complaint process, including credit repair organizations and those who offer credit advice online and on social media platforms” (CFPB, FCRA 611(e) report, December 2025). That’s the regulator’s assessment, reported as theirs. Not ours. We have no state-level data on third-party filing, so we make no state-level claim about it.

What this means for your file

Nothing, and that’s not a dodge. It’s the actual answer.

Your zip code doesn’t set your score. Not at all. Georgia’s complaint rate is a fact about Georgia’s filing behavior, not about any Georgian’s credit report. A high-complaint state doesn’t mean your report is more likely to be wrong. A low-complaint state doesn’t mean it’s clean. South Dakota’s 1.26 could just as easily mean nobody there is checking.

That’s worth sitting with. A low complaint rate is not evidence of a low error rate. It’s evidence of a low complaint rate.

Method

Complaints. CFPB Consumer Complaint Database, bulk CSV, https://files.consumerfinance.gov/ccdb/complaints.csv.zip. Pulled 2026-07-16. SHA256 9746821f7bfffb09947cab3e1d9acb3400edabb78388c8fbe70ff71f35aaaf48. Filtered to credit reporting products (both product labels the CFPB has used), Date received in calendar 2025.

Population. US Census Bureau, Vintage 2025 State Population Estimates (NST-EST2025), POPESTIMATE2025, the July 1 2025 estimate. Pulled 2026-07-16. SHA256 92188e29cb0a67dcf95afa7d6c47359409782f086478b70ea4128eb70e223ca9. Validated: 50 states plus DC sum to the published US total exactly.

Credit scores. Used only for the correlation. Average FICO by state, the dataset already published on this site, originally sourced from Experian.

US totals. 4,802,387 complaints attributed to the 50 states plus DC, over those same jurisdictions’ 341,784,857 residents, = 14.05 per 1,000. Numerator and denominator cover the identical 51 jurisdictions. Puerto Rico, the territories and the military ZIPs (AA, AE, AP) are excluded from both sides. Complaints with a blank state are excluded from the map.

Why 2025. The file ends 2026-07-16, so 2026 is a partial year. Dividing half a year of complaints by a full year of population would understate every state by roughly half.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which state files the most credit complaints per person?

Georgia, at 33.93 credit reporting complaints per 1,000 residents in 2025. Mississippi is second at 32.19 and Florida third at 30.99. The national rate is 14.05 per 1,000.

Which state files the fewest?

South Dakota and Vermont are tied at the bottom at 1.26 per 1,000, with Wyoming and Montana at 1.31. A Georgian is about 27 times more likely to file than a South Dakotan.

Does living in a certain state hurt your credit score?

No. Your address does not set your score. Payment history, how much of your credit you use, credit age, mix of accounts and recent inquiries decide it. A state's complaint rate is a fact about that state's filing behavior, not about your file.

Why does Georgia file so many credit complaints?

We do not know, and we are not going to guess. Average credit score correlates with the complaint rate at r = -0.675, which explains about 46% of the variance between states. That leaves most of the gap unexplained. Credit quality varies about 1.09x between states while complaint rates vary 27x, so score cannot carry the magnitude.

Is this counting people or complaints?

Complaints. One person can file about the same account against all three bureaus, which counts as three. So this measures filing volume per resident, not the share of residents with a problem.
