Credit Repair in North Carolina: Laws, Data, and Your Rights

North Carolina filed 15.48 credit complaints per 1,000 residents in 2025, above the US rate. Violating the state's credit repair law is a felony. The facts.

Alexander Katsman

July 26, 2026

North Carolina files more credit complaints than the national rate

North Carolinians filed 15.48 credit reporting complaints per 1,000 residents with the CFPB in 2025, above the national 14.05 and 14th of 51 jurisdictions in our credit complaints by state study.

Geography matters here: North Carolina sits on the edge of the Southeast’s complaint belt. Georgia, two borders away, leads the nation at 33.93 per 1,000. Virginia, directly north, files at 9.82. North Carolina lands between its neighbors, closer to the national average than to either extreme.

The study’s honest caveat applies here as everywhere: complaint rates measure filing behavior, not error rates. Above-average filing is not evidence that North Carolina reports carry more mistakes. Your report answers only to a reading.

What the CFPB data shows for North Carolina

Method, once, for all states: every credit reporting complaint in the CFPB’s public database for calendar 2025, divided by the state’s July 2025 Census population estimate.

North Carolina couples slightly elevated filing with a slightly depressed score: 707 against the national 713. Our study found average score explains less than half of the complaint-rate differences between states. The rest is unexplained, and this page will not pretend otherwise.

North Carolina credit repair law: a bond, a 3-day exit, and felony teeth

The federal floor. The Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA, 15 U.S.C. 1679 and following) applies in all 50 states: no charging before services are fully performed, a written contract, at least 3 business days to cancel, no untrue or misleading claims, and a mandatory written disclosure of your right to dispute inaccurate information in your credit report yourself, directly with the bureaus, free.

The North Carolina layer. The Credit Repair Services Act, N.C. Gen. Stat. 66-220 through 66-226, adds:

  • A $10,000 bond or trust account. Under G.S. 66-222, a credit repair business must hold a surety bond or a North Carolina trust account before taking money ahead of performance.
  • A 3 business day cancellation right. Under G.S. 66-224, you may cancel until midnight of the third business day, refund within 10 days.
  • No pay-before-performance, no false claims. G.S. 66-223 bans collecting before full and complete performance, referral-only fees, claims of removing accurate negative information, and false statements to bureaus or creditors.
  • Two hammers at once. Under G.S. 66-225, a violation is an unfair trade practice under G.S. 75-1.1 and a Class I felony, and the consumer can void the contract, recover all sums paid, and collect attorney fees.
  • No escaping across state lines. G.S. 66-226 applies the Act to any contract where solicitation, negotiation, signing, or performance happens in North Carolina.

There is no registration roster in Raleigh, so the practical vetting question for any company is: show the bond. And beneath it all, fixing your own credit is free, legal, and needs no bond, in North Carolina or anywhere.

The average credit score in North Carolina

North Carolina’s average FICO Score is 707 as of September 2025, per Experian, down two points from 709 a year earlier, the same two-point fall the national average took to 713, its first decline since 2013.

Six points under the national mean describes 11 million people at once, which is to say it describes nobody in particular. The full ranking is in our average credit score by state breakdown.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is credit repair legal in North Carolina?

Yes. Working on your own credit is always legal and free, and paid companies may operate under the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act plus North Carolina’s Credit Repair Services Act, N.C. Gen. Stat. 66-220 through 66-226. The state enforces it hard: a violation is both an unfair trade practice and a Class I felony.

Do credit repair companies in North Carolina need a bond?

Yes. N.C. Gen. Stat. 66-222 requires a $10,000 surety bond or a North Carolina trust account before charging consumers ahead of full performance. There is no state registration roster, so asking a company to show its bond is the practical check.

Can I cancel a credit repair contract in North Carolina?

Yes. You may cancel until midnight of the third business day after signing, with a refund due within 10 days of your notice. And if a company violates the Act, N.C. law goes further: you can void the contract entirely and recover everything you paid, plus attorney fees.

What happens to companies that break North Carolina’s credit repair law?

Two things at once. A violation counts as an unfair trade practice under G.S. 75-1.1, which carries treble damages exposure, and it is separately a Class I felony under G.S. 66-225. The Act also reaches any company whose solicitation, negotiation, signing, or performance touches North Carolina.

Can I fix my credit myself in North Carolina for free?

Yes. Federal law requires every credit repair company to tell you in writing that you can dispute inaccurate information yourself by contacting the credit bureaus directly. Reports are free at AnnualCreditReport.com, and the do-it-yourself route is legal in every state.

What is the average credit score in North Carolina?

707, per Experian data as of September 2025, six points below the national average of 713. North Carolina slipped two points from 709 a year earlier, matching the national decline, the first drop in the US average since 2013.
