Is StellarFi Legit? 2026 Safety and Cost Breakdown

Is StellarFi legit or a scam? It is a real, funded Public Benefit Corporation, not a scam. But it changed products in 2026. The safety, cost, and honest caveat.

Alexander Katsman

July 20, 2026

Is StellarFi Legit? Yes, and Here Is the Proof

StellarFi is legit, not a scam. It’s a real Austin company, a certified Public Benefit Corporation, backed by a $15 million Series A from Acrew Capital, and it reports to the credit bureaus. Scams don’t get funded by real VCs and structure themselves around a public benefit charter. StellarFi did both.

So the scam worry is the wrong worry. The real thing to know before you sign up is that StellarFi changed. It’s not the bill-reporting service the older reviews describe. Let’s break down safety, cost, and that change.

Is Your Money and Data Safe With StellarFi?

On safety, the picture is reassuring. StellarFi is a funded, mission-driven company, and its current Smart Card is issued through a regulated bank partner. You’re not handing your details to an anonymous website. You’re dealing with a real fintech that has investors, a charter, and a track record since 2021.

As with any app you connect to your bank, take the normal precautions. Use a strong password, and read the current terms. That’s true of every credit product, not a StellarFi-specific red flag.

The one thing to actually watch isn’t safety, it’s flux. StellarFi went through a banking-partner transition, and during it, reporting was briefly limited to a single bureau. So confirm what’s live right now before you count on it. The risk here is product confusion, NOT a shady operator.

What StellarFi Actually Costs

The current pricing is clean, and worth stating clearly because bad numbers are everywhere online:

  • Silver, secured: $0 a year.
  • Gold, semi-secured: $49 annual fee.
  • Stellarite, unsecured: $99 annual fee.

That $0 Silver tier has no hidden monthly charge. It’s a secured card that reports to all three bureaus at no annual cost, which is a fair, honest deal.

Here’s the trap to avoid. The old bill-reporting subscription had $4.99-to-$29.99 monthly tiers, and those figures still show up in outdated reviews. They no longer apply. If a page is quoting you a monthly StellarFi subscription for bill reporting, it’s describing a product that’s been retired.

The Real Caveat: It Changed What It Does

Here’s the concession, and it’s the whole story. StellarFi is legit, but it’s not what it was.

The original pitch, pay your bills and we report them as credit, is gone. StellarFi moved to the Smart Card, a credit card that builds history through usage. So if the reason you looked it up was bill reporting, the honest truth is that StellarFi left that game. A dedicated rent reporter or Experian Boost is a closer fit for reporting payments you already make.

And like every builder, it only adds. It can’t scan your report for errors or challenge a wrong late payment. If your score is low because something’s broken on your file, StellarFi’s card doesn’t touch it. That’s a job for Credit Booster AI, which reads all three reports and drafts the challenge for $9.99 a month.

The Verdict: Legit, Safe, but Not What It Was

StellarFi is legit and safe, and the current $0 secured card from a Public Benefit Corporation is a genuinely decent option. If you want a mission-driven card that reports to all three bureaus, it’s worth a look.

Just go in with clear eyes. The bill-reporting product is retired, the old monthly prices don’t apply, and it doesn’t fix errors. Match the current StellarFi to what you actually need, not the one the old reviews are selling.
