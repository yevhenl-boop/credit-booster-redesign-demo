StellarFi Review 2026: Is Bill Reporting Worth It?

StellarFi is legit, but it largely left bill reporting behind and became a credit card in 2026. What it does now, what it costs, and whether it is worth it.

Alexander Katsman

July 20, 2026

Is StellarFi Bill Reporting Worth It? First, It Changed

Here’s the thing most reviews haven’t caught up to. StellarFi is legit, but it’s not really a bill-reporting service anymore. In 2026 it transitioned to a credit card, and the original “pay your bills, we report them” subscription got kicked to the side.

So the honest answer to “is StellarFi bill reporting worth it” starts with a correction: that product has largely moved on. What’s left is a Smart Card that builds credit through card usage.

That’s still a real, legit tool from a mission-driven company. But if your actual goal was to turn bills you already pay into credit, StellarFi is no longer the clean fit. Credit Booster AI still does that, reporting rent and bills, and it also scans all three bureaus for errors and drafts the challenge, for $9.99 a month.

What Is StellarFi Now?

StellarFi launched in 2021 out of Austin with a genuinely clever pitch: link your recurring bills, StellarFi pays them through a line, and it reports those on-time payments as a credit tradeline. It’s a certified Public Benefit Corporation, backed by a $15 million Series A led by Acrew Capital. The mission is real.

But the product changed. StellarFi transitioned to its Smart Card, a digital credit card issued through a bank partner that builds credit the way any card does, through usage and on-time payment. The pure bill-reporting subscription has been retired. During the banking-partner switch, some reporting was even temporarily limited to Experian, with the full setup relaunching on the card.

What that means for you is simple. If you came for bill reporting, that’s not the current StellarFi. If you came for a credit-building card with a Public Benefit mission, that’s exactly what it is now. Just know which product you’re actually signing up for, because a lot of older reviews describe a StellarFi that no longer exists.

StellarFi Pricing in 2026

The Smart Card has three tiers:

  • Silver, secured: $0 a year.
  • Gold, semi-secured: $49 annual fee.
  • Stellarite, unsecured: $99 annual fee.

Ignore the old $4.99-to-$29.99 monthly subscription numbers you’ll still find quoted around the web. Those were the retired bill-reporting tiers. And because the product is mid-transition, confirm the current pricing on StellarFi’s own site before you commit, since the details have been moving.

The $0 Silver tier is the honest highlight. A secured card that reports to all three bureaus with no annual fee is a fair deal, and the Public Benefit Corporation structure is a real point in its favor if mission matters to you.

What StellarFi Can’t Do

Here’s the concession. StellarFi builds history through a card. It can’t fix your report, and it no longer turns your existing bills into credit.

It has no feature to scan your reports for errors or to challenge a wrong late payment or a collection. And the bill-reporting angle that made it interesting in the first place is gone. So if your score is low because something’s broken on your report, a new card tradeline doesn’t address it.

Who StellarFi Is Best For

StellarFi still has a real audience, just a narrower one than before.

  • Best for a mission-minded card starter: If you want a secured card at $0 a year from a Public Benefit Corporation, the Silver tier is a solid, honest choice. Real win.
  • Best for building through card usage: If you’ll use a card responsibly, the Smart Card reports to all three bureaus.
  • Skip it if: you specifically wanted bill reporting, which StellarFi retired, or your score is low from errors rather than a thin file. In those cases the card model isn’t the answer.

The Verdict on StellarFi

StellarFi is legit, and the Public Benefit mission plus a $0 secured card make the current product worth a look for the right person. Three-bureau reporting on the Smart Card is real credit building.

But answer the question you came with honestly. As a bill-reporting service, StellarFi has largely left the building. If that’s what you wanted, it’s no longer the tool, and older reviews selling you on the bill-pay model are describing the past.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is StellarFi legit?

Yes. StellarFi is a real US company, a certified Public Benefit Corporation founded in 2021 and based in Austin, Texas, that raised a $15 million Series A led by Acrew Capital. It reports on-time activity to the credit bureaus. It is legit. The wrinkle in 2026 is that StellarFi has largely moved away from its original bill-reporting subscription and toward a credit card product, so what it is has changed.

Does StellarFi still report your bills to build credit?

Mostly no, and this matters. StellarFi's original model paid your bills and reported them as a tradeline, but the company has transitioned to its Smart Card credit-card product, and the pure bill-reporting subscription has been kicked to the side. If your specific goal is to report existing bills you already pay, StellarFi is no longer the clean fit it once was, and a rent reporter or Experian Boost may be more on point.

How much does StellarFi cost in 2026?

The current Smart Card lineup has three tiers: Silver, a secured card, at $0 a year; Gold, semi-secured, at a $49 annual fee; and Stellarite, unsecured, at a $99 annual fee. These replaced the old bill-reporting subscription tiers, so ignore any older $4.99 to $29.99 monthly figures you see quoted, and confirm the current pricing on StellarFi's site since the product is in transition.

Does StellarFi report to all three bureaus?

The Smart Card reports to all three, Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. Worth knowing: the retired bill-reporting model reported to only Experian and Equifax, not TransUnion, and during the banking-partner transition some reporting was limited to Experian. So the all-three coverage applies to the card, and details have been in flux, which is a reason to verify before you rely on it.

Is StellarFi bill reporting worth it?

For the original promise, turning your existing bills into credit history, StellarFi is no longer really the tool, because it moved to a card. If you want a credit card that builds history and you like the Public Benefit Corporation mission, the Smart Card can be worth it, especially the $0 Silver tier. But if your actual goal is reporting bills or rent you already pay, look at a dedicated rent reporter or a builder that still does that.
