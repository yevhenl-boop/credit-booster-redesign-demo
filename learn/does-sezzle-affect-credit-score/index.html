Does Sezzle Affect Your Credit Score? 2026 Facts

Standard Sezzle does not touch your credit score, but Sezzle Up does: opt in and it reports your payments to all three bureaus. Here is the 2026 breakdown.

Alexander Katsman

July 20, 2026

Does Sezzle Affect Your Credit Score? Only If You Flip One Switch

Regular Sezzle does not touch your credit score. Sezzle Up does. That one distinction is the whole answer, and most articles blur it into a vague “it depends.”

Here is the clean version. The four-payment plan you tap at checkout, plain Sezzle, is not reported to the credit bureaus at all. Pay it perfectly for a year and your score never hears about it. Turn on Sezzle Up, though, and Sezzle starts reporting your payment history to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian, where it can actually build credit.

So Sezzle is the rare buy now, pay later app that CAN move your number. But only on purpose, only after you opt in, and only if you keep paying on time.

The Two Sezzles, and Why They Are Not the Same

People think of Sezzle as one thing. For your credit, it is two.

Standard Sezzle is the default. It splits a purchase into four interest-free payments, runs a soft check that never dents your score, and reports nothing to the bureaus. Invisible in both directions. It cannot help you and, on its own, it cannot hurt you.

Sezzle Up is the opt-in upgrade. Enable it and you are telling Sezzle to send your payment history to the bureaus. That is the version credit-builders want, because a reported tradeline is the only kind that a score can read.

Here is the concession the “Sezzle builds credit!” crowd skips. Turning on Sezzle Up cuts both ways. The same pipe that reports your on-time payments reports a payment that goes 30 days overdue. You are trading invisibility for the chance to build, and the chance to slip.

What Sezzle Up Actually Reports

This is where Sezzle earns its spot as the outlier among BNPL apps.

Unlike the walled-off buy now, pay later data the bureaus keep out of scoring, Sezzle Up furnishes your eligible payments as an installment tradeline to your core credit file. Sezzle’s own help pages say enrolling means “opting to report your payment history to the credit bureaus,” and it lists Equifax, TransUnion and Experian, plus Innovis.

The timing catches people off guard. It can take up to about 60 days from your first order after signing up for the account to surface on a report. So if you enrolled last week and your score has not budged, nothing is broken. Give it a cycle or two.

And the reporting is honest in both directions. On-time payments, meaning nothing outstanding beyond 30 days, are reported as on time. A payment that sits 30 days overdue gets reported starting that month. No hiding the bad with the good.

The One Way Standard Sezzle Can Still Hurt You

Forget Sezzle Up for a second, because there is a risk that ignores the whole opt-in question.

Stop paying a Sezzle balance long enough and it does not matter which version you used. The debt can be sold or assigned to a collection agency, and that agency reports a collection. A collection is not tagged as buy now, pay later. It is ordinary derogatory data, and it hits your file like any other, over a pair of shoes you split into four payments.

So the real shape of Sezzle risk is lopsided. Standard Sezzle paid on time does nothing to your score. Standard Sezzle abandoned can leave a collection that follows you for years. The danger was never the plan. It was falling behind on it.

What This Means For Your File

If you actually want to build credit, Sezzle Up is a legitimate, free way to start, and that is more than we can say for Affirm, Klarna or Afterpay in 2026. But keep it in proportion. It reports short installments, so it does not build much account age, and its effect on a real score is modest.

The bigger levers are already sitting on your report: your balances, your oldest accounts, the late marks, and the errors. That last one matters, because errors are common and they are the only negative you can actually get removed.

The Verdict

Does Sezzle affect your credit score? Not unless you turn on Sezzle Up. Standard Sezzle is invisible to the bureaus, so it neither helps nor hurts on its own. Sezzle Up reports your payments to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian, which is real credit building and real risk in the same switch.

Two things to take with you. One, plain Sezzle builds nothing, so stop expecting it to. Two, any unpaid Sezzle balance can still become a collection, and that has nothing to do with the opt-in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Sezzle affect your credit score?

Only if you turn on Sezzle Up. Regular Sezzle Pay in 4 is not reported to the credit bureaus, so it does not move your score up or down. Sezzle Up is the opt-in version, and when you enable it Sezzle reports your payment history to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian, where it can build credit or, if you fall behind, hurt it.

Does standard Sezzle build credit?

No. Plain Sezzle, the four-payment plan most people use at checkout, is not furnished to any bureau, so a perfect record on it builds nothing. If your goal is credit, you have to opt into Sezzle Up. Signing up for Sezzle and paying on time without Sezzle Up does nothing for your score.

Does Sezzle do a hard credit check?

No. Sezzle uses a soft check to decide your spending power, and a soft check does not affect your credit score and is not visible to other lenders. That is true whether or not you use Sezzle Up. The reporting that Sezzle Up turns on is about your payment history going out, not a hard inquiry coming in.

Can Sezzle hurt my credit score?

Yes, in two ways. If you are on Sezzle Up and a payment goes 30 days overdue, Sezzle reports that late mark and it can drag your score. Separate from that, any Sezzle balance you stop paying can be sold to a collection agency, and that collection lands on your report the normal way whether or not you ever used Sezzle Up.

Which bureaus does Sezzle report to?

For Sezzle Up users in the US, Sezzle reports to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian, and it also lists Innovis. It can take up to about 60 days from your first order after signing up for the account to appear on your credit report, so do not panic if you do not see it right away.

Is Sezzle Up worth it for building credit?

It can help a thin file, because it reports as an installment tradeline to the core credit file rather than a walled-off buy now, pay later section. But the effect is modest and it depends on you paying every plan on time. A secured card or a dedicated credit builder that reports to all three bureaus usually does more, and misses hurt here just like anywhere else.
