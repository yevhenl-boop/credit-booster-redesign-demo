Which BNPL Apps Report to Credit Bureaus in 2026?

A verified table of which buy now pay later apps report to the credit bureaus: Affirm, Klarna, Afterpay, Sezzle, Zip and PayPal, what each sends, in 2026.

Which BNPL Apps Report to Credit Bureaus? The 2026 Short List

Three do, in different ways: Affirm, Klarna’s monthly plans, and Sezzle Up. The rest mostly do not. That is the fast answer, and the table below is the verified detail.

The reporting is uneven on purpose, and it changed a lot in 2025, so any list older than that is stale. Affirm reports the most. Klarna reports only its longer financing. Sezzle reports if you opt in. Afterpay, Zip and PayPal Pay in 4 report nothing on-time. And even where a provider reports, most of that data is not counted in the score lenders pull.

The Master Reporting Table

The table below is compiled from each provider’s own reporting statements and the bureaus’ buy now, pay later policies, verified in July 2026. Two rows carry the most weight: which bureaus each one furnishes to, and which of them actually counts inside the FICO Score 8 lenders pull. Only Sezzle Up gets a clear yes on that last row.

How to Read It: Furnishing Is Not Scoring

The one idea that makes this table useful is that reporting and scoring are two separate steps.

Furnishing means the provider sends your data to a bureau. Affirm, Klarna’s monthly plans and Sezzle Up all do this. Scoring means a score a lender pulls actually counts that data. This is where almost all of them fall out, because the bureaus tag buy now, pay later data and keep it out of the FICO Score 8.

Sezzle Up is the exception that proves the rule. It reports to your core file as an installment tradeline, the same place a small loan lands, so a score can read it. Affirm and Klarna report, but their data is walled off and not scored yet. So “reports to the bureaus” and “helps your score” are not the same claim, and this table separates them deliberately.

Provider Notes, Kept Honest

A few specifics that the grid cannot hold.

Affirm reports all pay-over-time plans, including Pay in 4, to Experian and TransUnion, for loans from April and May 2025. Not Equifax. Some approvals route through partner lenders and involve a hard credit check.

Klarna reports its longer monthly financing to Experian and TransUnion, and holds Pay in 4 back, saying the bureaus lack proper models to process it responsibly.

Afterpay does not report on-time buy now, pay later activity, beyond a limited Experian opt-in pilot. It has said it will not until it sees evidence the data helps rather than hurts customers’ scores.

Sezzle reports only if you enroll in Sezzle Up or use long-term financing, and then to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian.

Zip and PayPal Pay in 4 do not report on-time activity at all. PayPal Credit, a separate revolving line issued by Synchrony Bank, does report, but that is not Pay in 4.

Here is the concession the table cannot show: this is a snapshot. Reporting has moved every year since 2024, and it will move again. Confirm your own account rather than trusting any provider’s policy to stay put.

What Every Row Shares

One thing is true across the entire table, reported or not.

A missed payment can reach collections at any of them. While you pay, most of these plans are invisible. Default, and the balance can be sold to a collection agency, which reports a collection under its own name. That collection is scored, it is on your report, and it hits your file like any other. So the bottom row is the same for all six: the plan may be quiet, but the default is not.

What This Means For Your File

Use this table to set expectations, not to build a credit plan. If you want your buy now, pay later to count, that means Sezzle Up, opted in and paid on time, and even that is modest. For real building, a secured card or credit builder loan reports and is scored today.

The Verdict

Which BNPL apps report to credit bureaus in 2026? Affirm and Klarna’s monthly plans, to Experian and TransUnion, and Sezzle Up, to all three, if you opt in. Afterpay, Zip and PayPal Pay in 4 report no on-time activity. And only Sezzle Up’s reporting is actually scored today.

Two takeaways. One, reporting is not the same as helping your score, so read the last row of the table, not the first. Two, this is a snapshot, so confirm your own account.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which BNPL apps report to credit bureaus in 2026?

Affirm reports all its plans to Experian and TransUnion. Klarna reports only its longer monthly financing, to Experian and TransUnion, not Pay in 4. Sezzle reports if you opt into Sezzle Up, to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian. Afterpay, Zip and PayPal Pay in 4 do not report on-time activity. So the ones that report are Affirm, Klarna’s monthly plans, and Sezzle Up; the rest are mostly invisible unless you default.

Does any BNPL report to all three bureaus?

Sezzle Up is the closest, reporting to Equifax, TransUnion and Experian for enrolled users. Affirm and Klarna report to Experian and TransUnion, but not Equifax. So if you want three-bureau coverage from a buy now, pay later product specifically, Sezzle Up is the one that spans all three, and only for its reportable activity.

Does reporting mean the BNPL counts in my credit score?

Not usually. Most reported buy now, pay later data is tagged by the bureaus and kept out of the FICO Score 8 lenders pull, so it is on your report without being scored. The exception is Sezzle Up, which reports to your core file as an installment tradeline a score can read. Affirm and Klarna report, but that data is not scored yet.

Which BNPL apps do not report to credit bureaus?

Afterpay, Zip and PayPal Pay in 4 do not report your on-time activity, and Klarna does not report its Pay in 4 plans. A perfect record on any of those builds nothing because it never reaches your file. They can still hurt you in one way: a balance sent to collections is reported by the collection agency, not the BNPL app.

Is this reporting list likely to change?

Yes. Buy now, pay later reporting has shifted a lot since 2024 and it continues to move, provider by provider. Affirm expanded reporting in 2025, and others may follow. So treat this table as a July 2026 snapshot, and confirm your own account rather than assuming a provider’s policy is fixed.

How do I check if my BNPL is on my credit report?

Pull all three of your credit reports and look for a tradeline under the provider’s name, usually an installment account. A collection shows under a collection agency instead. Because reporting is uneven and some data sits in a separate bureau, not seeing it does not always mean it is absent, so read all three.
