How Credit Monitoring Actually Works

A monitor connects to one or more of your bureau files and reports back two things: your score and any change to the accounts behind it. When a new account opens, a balance jumps, or a collection appears, the app pings you. That early warning is genuinely useful, especially for catching identity theft fast.

But look at what the number actually is. Credit Karma and WalletHub show a VantageScore, not a FICO, so it can read higher or lower than the FICO a lender pulls. Experian’s free app shows a real FICO Score 8, and myFICO shows real FICO across all three on paid tiers. The scores differ because they are built by different models on different data, which is normal, and it is why chasing one exact number across apps is a waste of energy.

Now the ceiling, and it is the whole point of this page. A monitor changes nothing. It cannot remove the wrong collection it just alerted you to. It cannot dispute the duplicate account it flagged. It cannot pay your balance down. About one in four people find an error on a report that could be costing them points, per the FTC’s accuracy study, and a monitor will show you that error and then wait. Fixing it is a separate action under a separate law: you dispute it, the bureau gets 30 days to investigate, and if it is wrong, it comes off. That is the step Credit Booster AI takes for you, reading all three files, drafting the letters, and tracking the response window, so the alert turns into a correction instead of a notification you scroll past.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best credit monitoring app in 2026?

For breadth on a free plan, Credit Karma is the best credit monitoring app because it covers two bureaus, Equifax and TransUnion, at no cost with alerts and a score simulator. Experian’s free app is the best pick if you want a real FICO Score 8 from an actual bureau, though it shows only your Experian file. WalletHub is best for free daily updates on TransUnion. myFICO is the paid choice for real FICO scores across all three bureaus. All of them show you the problem. If you want the problem fixed, not just flagged, Credit Booster AI reads all three and drafts the removal letters.

What is the best free credit monitoring app?

There is no single winner, because every free monitor is missing a bureau. Credit Karma is free and covers Equifax plus TransUnion, but not Experian. Experian’s free app shows a real FICO Score 8, but only for Experian. WalletHub is free with daily updates, but TransUnion only. The honest move is to run two free apps that cover different bureaus, for example Credit Karma plus Experian, so you can see all three files without paying. Just remember that watching all three still does not fix anything on any of them.

Is Credit Karma accurate?

The data is real, but the score is not the one most lenders use. Credit Karma shows a VantageScore 3.0 built from your Equifax and TransUnion files, not a FICO score, so the number can sit above or below the FICO a lender actually pulls. That gap is normal and does not mean either is wrong. Credit Karma is accurate for spotting changes and new accounts on two bureaus. For the score a mortgage or auto lender reads, you want a real FICO from Experian’s free app or from myFICO.

Which app shows my real FICO score?

Two do it well. Experian’s free app shows a real FICO Score 8 based on your Experian file, at no cost, from the bureau itself. myFICO shows real FICO scores across all three bureaus on its paid tiers, including the mortgage and auto versions lenders pull for those specific loans. Credit Karma and WalletHub both show VantageScore, not FICO. If a single free FICO is enough, start with Experian. If you need all three FICOs before a big application, myFICO is the paid option.

Does credit monitoring improve your score?

No, and this is the concession the monitoring apps do not lead with. Watching a number does not move it. A monitor shows you what is on your report and alerts you when it changes, but the score only rises when the report underneath it changes, which means correcting errors, paying down balances, and adding on-time history. If you check your score every day and do nothing else, it will sit exactly where it is. Monitoring is the dashboard. It is not the engine.

How many bureaus should I monitor?

All three, because lenders can pull any of them and an error often sits on only one. That is the trap with free monitors: each one covers a different slice, so a single free app always leaves a blind spot. Credit Karma misses Experian, WalletHub is TransUnion only, and Experian’s free app shows only Experian. You can cover all three for free by combining apps, or pay for three-bureau access. Better still, use a tool that reads all three and then acts on what it finds, which is what Credit Booster AI is built to do.
