The 8 Best Credit Score Apps of 2026

The best credit score app of 2026 is Capital One CreditWise: free, a real FICO Score 8 from TransUnion, updated as often as daily, no account required.

Alexander Katsman

September 11, 2026•9 min read

The best credit score app of 2026 is Capital One CreditWise, because it is the only genuinely free app that shows a real FICO Score 8, refreshes as often as daily, and does not require an account with the issuer. Credit Karma is the better free pick if you want two bureaus instead of one, and myFICO at $19.95 to $39.95 a month is the only app here that shows the mortgage and auto FICO versions lenders actually pull.

Key takeaways

  • The best credit score app for September 2026 is Capital One CreditWise, because it is free, shows a real FICO Score 8 built from your TransUnion credit report, and updates as often as every day.
  • Most free apps do not show FICO. Credit Karma, WalletHub and Chase Credit Journey all display a VantageScore, which usually reads differently from the FICO a lender pulls.
  • myFICO is the only app on this list that sells the mortgage and auto FICO versions, at $19.95, $29.95 or $39.95 a month.
  • No score app changes your report. Correcting an error is a separate legal process under FCRA Section 611, which gives the bureau 30 days to reinvestigate.
  • Credit Booster AI is $9.99 a month for Plus, $29.99 for Pro and $99.99 for Max, with a 7-day free trial on Plus only and no free tier.

How we ranked the best credit score apps

We reviewed 10 consumer credit score apps and ranked 8. For each one we recorded 11 data points: monthly price, annual cost, free tier or trial length, bureaus behind the score, bureaus covered for report access, score model and version, refresh frequency, whether an account with the issuer is required, whether the app can act on an error it finds, whether it includes a score simulator, and whether it includes identity theft or dark web monitoring.

Each app was scored against six weighted categories:

  • Score model honesty, 25%. Does the app show the score a lender actually pulls, and does it say plainly which model and version it is?
  • Bureau coverage, 20%. One bureau, two, or all three.
  • Cost and value, 20%. Price against what the tier actually unlocks.
  • Refresh frequency, 15%. Daily beats monthly, because a wrong item costs you points every day it sits there.
  • Ability to act, 12%. Can the app do anything about what it finds, or does it only report?
  • Extras, 8%. Simulator, alerts, identity theft insurance.

One honest limit on this edition: it is a documentation review, not a hands-on lab test. Every price, score model and refresh interval below came from the provider’s own live page, pulled 11 September 2026, and cross-checked against a second page from the same provider where one existed. We did not run multi-month in-app trials for this edition, so we publish no alert counts, no screenshots and no test durations, because we did not observe them.

Best credit score app overall: Capital One CreditWise

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free. Bureau behind the score: TransUnion. Score model: FICO Score 8. Refresh: as often as daily. Free tier: yes, no card required. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. The only free app here that pairs a real FICO Score 8 with a near-daily refresh. Capital One states you do not need a Capital One account, which now includes Discover card accounts, to sign up. Includes a score simulator and dark web monitoring at no cost.

Cons. One bureau only. An error sitting on your Equifax or Experian file is invisible here.

Why we chose it. Most roundups still describe CreditWise as a VantageScore product. That is out of date. Capital One’s own CreditWise FAQ now states that “your FICO Score 8 is updated as frequently as each day,” drawn from your TransUnion credit report. A free app showing the model that myFICO says 90% of top lenders use is the strongest free offer in the category.

Best free credit score app for two-bureau coverage: Credit Karma

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free. Bureaus behind the score: Equifax and TransUnion. Score model: VantageScore 3.0. Refresh: continual. Free tier: yes. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. Two of your three files at no cost, with alerts, a score simulator and approval-odds tooling. The widest free coverage of any single app.

Cons. Not FICO. Credit Karma states directly that its scores use VantageScore 3.0 and come from Equifax and TransUnion. Experian is not covered at all.

Why we chose it. Breadth. If you only install one free app and you care about seeing changes across more than one bureau, this is it. Just do not treat the number as the one a lender will read. We break down the gap in FICO vs VantageScore and in what score Credit Karma shows.

Best credit score app for a thin file: Experian

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free. Bureau behind the score: Experian. Score model: FICO Score 8. Refresh: Experian credit report updated daily. Free tier: yes, no credit card required. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. A real FICO Score 8 from the bureau itself, free, with a daily-updating report. Experian Boost is included at no cost and adds on-time phone, utility, rent, insurance, internet and streaming payments from your bank history to your Experian file.

Cons. Experian only. The three-bureau view sits behind a paid upgrade.

Why we chose it. A thin file fails to score because there is not enough reported history behind it, and Boost is the only free tool that adds real payment history you are already making. Experian states that most people who qualify get an instant increase in their FICO Score by an average of 13 points. That is Experian’s published figure, not a promise, and it will not apply to everyone. More options in best credit app for a thin file.

Best credit score app that shows the FICO versions lenders pull: myFICO

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free 1-bureau tier, then $19.95 (Basic, Experian), $29.95 (Advanced, 3 bureaus), $39.95 (Premier, 3 bureaus). Score model: FICO Score 8 plus mortgage and auto versions. Refresh: monthly on Free, Basic and Premier, every 3 months on Advanced. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. The only app here that shows the version-specific scores a mortgage or auto lender reads. myFICO names them: FICO Score 5 at Equifax, FICO Score 4 at TransUnion and FICO Score 2 at Experian for mortgages, and FICO Score 8 as the version most widely used elsewhere.

Cons. Expensive for what it is, and the tier pricing is counterintuitive. Advanced costs $29.95 a month but refreshes only every 3 months, so a full year of Advanced is $359.40 for four three-bureau refreshes, about $89.85 per refresh.

Why we chose it. Before a mortgage or auto application, the version matters more than the number. Nothing else on this list sells it. Which version applies to you is covered in which FICO score matters most.

Best credit score app for daily updates: WalletHub

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free. Bureau behind the score: TransUnion. Score model: VantageScore 3.0. Refresh: daily. Free tier: yes. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. Free daily score and report refreshes, 24/7 monitoring and a score simulator, with plain explanations of what moved and why.

Cons. TransUnion only, and VantageScore rather than FICO.

Why we chose it. Speed. If you are paying down balances before an application, a daily refresh shows the utilization change sooner than a monthly one. Timing is explained in how often your credit score updates.

Best credit score app for bank customers: Chase Credit Journey

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: free. Bureau behind the score: Experian. Score model: VantageScore 3.0. Refresh: eligible every 7 days. Free tier: yes. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. Free Experian report access and alerts, and Chase confirms you do not need to be a Chase customer to enroll. Checking is a soft inquiry.

Cons. VantageScore 3.0, and Chase says so directly: your lenders, including Chase, may not use VantageScore 3.0, so the score they evaluate may differ from the one shown.

Why we chose it. It is the cleanest free way to see your Experian file alongside a second opinion on the score, and the weekly refresh cadence is faster than most free monthly products.

Best credit score app with identity theft protection: Aura

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: $15 a month, or $12 a month billed annually, for Individual. Couple is $29 monthly or $22 billed annually. Family is $50 monthly or $32 billed annually. Bureau behind the score: Equifax, with 3-bureau inquiry alerts. Score model: VantageScore, monthly. Free tier: none. Platforms: iOS, Android, web.

Pros. Three-bureau inquiry alerts, dark web monitoring and identity theft coverage in one subscription.

Cons. The score itself is the weakest part: one VantageScore from your Equifax file, once a month. Every free app above refreshes faster.

Why we chose it. Effective annual cost, derived: Individual is $144 a year against $180 paid monthly, so annual billing saves $36. Couple is $264 against $348. Family is $384 against $600. Buy Aura for the identity protection, not for the score.

Best credit score app for fixing credit report errors: Credit Booster AI

HIGHLIGHTS. Cost: Plus $9.99 a month, Pro $29.99, Max $99.99. Trial: 7 days, Plus tier only. Free tier: none. Coverage: reads all three credit reports. Score display: none, this is not a score viewer. Platforms: iOS, Android.

Pros. Reads all three reports, identifies items that are disputable, and drafts the dispute letters. Effective annual cost, derived: $119.88 for Plus, $359.88 for Pro, $1,199.88 for Max.

Cons. Nothing is free beyond the 7-day Plus trial, and no lender score appears on screen, so it does not replace a monitoring app. If your reports are accurate and clean, it is money you do not need to spend.

Why we chose it. Every other app on this list stops at telling you. A score only moves when the report underneath it changes, and correcting an inaccurate item runs on statute: FCRA Section 611, codified at 15 U.S.C. 1681i, requires the bureau to complete a reasonable reinvestigation within 30 days of receiving the dispute, extendable by no more than 15 additional days in one narrow circumstance. Accurate negative information stays on the report. Nobody can remove that, and any company promising otherwise is selling something it cannot deliver.

VantageScore or FICO: which one is the app actually showing you

This is the single most common source of confusion in the category, so here it is flatly. FICO and VantageScore are two different companies’ models. They read the same credit reports and produce different numbers, and both numbers are legitimate.

Of the apps on this list, CreditWise, Experian and myFICO show FICO. Credit Karma, WalletHub, Chase Credit Journey and Aura show VantageScore. myFICO states that 90% of top lenders use FICO Scores, and that for mortgages lenders typically use FICO Score 5 at Equifax, FICO Score 4 at TransUnion and FICO Score 2 at Experian, none of which any free app shows.

The CFPB settles the argument about which is “right” in Ask CFPB: “You do not have just ‘one’ credit score,” and each score “may differ depending on the scoring model, the source of the data used, and even the day when it was calculated.” Chasing an exact match across apps is wasted effort. Watching the direction of travel is not.

What a score app cannot do

The CFPB defines a credit monitoring service as “a commercial service that charges you a fee to watch your credit reports and alert you to changes to the accounts listed on your credit report.” Watching is the whole job description. None of these apps corrects anything.

Two free things sit outside the apps entirely. You can pull your full report from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion once every week at AnnualCreditReport.com, which the FTC confirms all three bureaus have permanently extended. And disputing an error is free and direct, with or without a paid tool. Compare the watching tools in best credit monitoring app 2026.

What changed recently

Last reviewed: 11 September 2026.

  • Capital One CreditWise now shows FICO, not VantageScore. Capital One’s CreditWise FAQ states the score is a FICO Score 8 from your TransUnion report, updated as often as daily. Discover’s own free credit score page now routes to CreditWise and notes that Discover card accounts count as Capital One accounts for eligibility.
  • Free weekly reports are permanent. The FTC states all three nationwide bureaus have permanently extended weekly free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com.
  • myFICO tier pricing still inverts. Advanced at $29.95 a month refreshes every 3 months, while Premier at $39.95 refreshes monthly. Verify the refresh interval, not just the price.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best credit score app in 2026?

Capital One CreditWise is the best free credit score app in 2026. It shows a real FICO Score 8 built from your TransUnion credit report, updates as often as daily, costs nothing, and does not require a Capital One or Discover account. Credit Karma is the better pick if you want two bureaus instead of one, and myFICO is the only app on this list that shows the mortgage and auto FICO versions across all three bureaus.

Which credit score app shows your real FICO score?

Three credit score apps show a real FICO score. Capital One CreditWise shows a FICO Score 8 from your TransUnion file for free. Experian's free membership shows a FICO Score 8 from your Experian file. myFICO shows FICO Score 8 plus the mortgage and auto versions across all three bureaus on paid plans priced at $19.95, $29.95 and $39.95 a month. Credit Karma, WalletHub and Chase Credit Journey all show VantageScore, not FICO.

Is the Credit Karma score the same as my FICO score?

No. Credit Karma shows a VantageScore 3.0 built from your Equifax and TransUnion files, while myFICO states that 90% of top lenders use FICO Scores. The two models read the same underlying reports with different math, so the numbers usually differ, and neither one is wrong. The CFPB puts it plainly in Ask CFPB: you do not have just one credit score.

Are free credit score apps actually free?

Yes. Capital One CreditWise, Credit Karma, WalletHub, Chase Credit Journey and Experian's basic membership are all free with no card required. The CFPB warns that before accepting any free credit monitoring offer you should check for hidden fees or cancellation requirements. You can also pull your full report from all three bureaus once a week at AnnualCreditReport.com, which the FTC confirms has been made permanent.
